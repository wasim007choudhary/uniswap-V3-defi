# Uniswap V3 — Active Liquidity Introduction

# Lesson Goal

This lesson answers one important question:

> **How does Uniswap V3 keep track of the currently active liquidity?**

Notice carefully.

The lesson is **not** talking about:

- Total liquidity in the pool ❌
- Liquidity owned by Alice ❌
- Liquidity owned by Bob ❌

Instead, it focuses on:

> **Current Active Liquidity**

# Multiple Liquidity Positions

Imagine several Liquidity Providers (LPs) have deposited liquidity.

For example,

```text
Alice

$2 ---------------- $8

Liquidity = 5
```

```text
Bob

       $4 ---------------- $10

Liquidity = 9
```

```text
Charlie

             $6 ---------------- $12

Liquidity = 4
```

```text
David

                    $8 ---------------- $15

Liquidity = 6
```

Every LP owns **their own liquidity position**.

Each position has:

- A lower tick
- An upper tick
- A liquidity amount (`L`)

# Price Movement During A Swap

This part of the lesson specifically describes **what happens during a swap**.

If

```text
Token X enters the Pool

↓

Token Y leaves the Pool
```

then

```text
Price moves Left (←)
```

If

```text
Token Y enters the Pool

↓

Token X leaves the Pool
```

then

```text
Price moves Right (→)
```

> **Important**
>
> These rules apply specifically during the execution of a **swap**.

---

# A Helpful Intuition

As the price moves,

the available liquidity also changes.

A useful way to think about it is:

### If the price moves Left

```text
X enters

↓

Y leaves

↓

Price moves Left
```

Since token **Y** is continually leaving the pool,

the available **Y liquidity keeps decreasing**.

---

### If the price moves Right

```text
Y enters

↓

X leaves

↓

Price moves Right
```

Since token **X** is continually leaving the pool,

the available **X liquidity keeps decreasing**.

This matches the concentrated liquidity curve:

- Left side of the current price → liquidity is entirely **Y**.
- Right side of the current price → liquidity is entirely **X**.

---

# Why Does Active Liquidity Change?

Suppose the current active liquidity is

```text
14
```

Now imagine the price moves to the left.

Some LPs are no longer active.

The active liquidity becomes

```text
4
```

Now imagine instead the price moves to the right.

The lesson gives the following example.

```text
14

↓

13

↓

8

↓

5

↓

0
```

Notice something.

The liquidity doesn't change smoothly.

It changes in **steps**.

This is because liquidity only changes when the current price crosses an **initialized tick**.

Between two initialized ticks,

the active liquidity remains constant.

# What Is Active Liquidity?

Suppose three LPs are currently active.

```text
Alice

Liquidity = 5
```

```text
Bob

Liquidity = 6
```

```text
Charlie

Liquidity = 3
```

The current active liquidity is simply

```text
5

+

6

+

3

=

14
```

That's all Active Liquidity means.

It is simply

> **The sum of every liquidity position that is currently active at the current price.**

# Key Takeaways

- Every LP has its own liquidity position.
- At any given price, only some LPs are active.
- Current Active Liquidity is simply the **sum of all active liquidity positions**.
- During a swap:
  - **X enters → Y leaves → Price moves Left (←)**
  - **Y enters → X leaves → Price moves Right (→)**
- If the price moves Left, the available **Y liquidity** keeps decreasing.
- If the price moves Right, the available **X liquidity** keeps decreasing.
- Active Liquidity changes only when the price crosses an initialized tick.
- Between initialized ticks, Active Liquidity remains constant.
- This lesson introduces **what Active Liquidity is**. The next lesson explains **how Uniswap V3 efficiently tracks it using `liquidityNet`.**
