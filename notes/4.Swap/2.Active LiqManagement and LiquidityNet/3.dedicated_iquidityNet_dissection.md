## Previous Lesson (*Active Liquidity Introduction.md*)

Suppose the current price intersects several liquidity positions.

The pool simply adds all of those active liquidity positions together.

For example,

```text
Alice     = 5

Bob       = 9

Charlie   = 4
```

If only Alice and Bob are active,

```text
Current Active Liquidity

=

5 + 9

=

14
```

Question:

> **Where did the number 14 come from?**

Answer:

It is simply the **sum of all liquidity positions that are currently active at the current price.**

That was the goal of the previous lesson.

## This Lesson

Now suppose the current Active Liquidity is

```text
Current Active Liquidity = 14
```

A swap happens.

The price moves.

Eventually, the price crosses an initialized tick.

Now the pool says

```text
Current Active Liquidity

14

↓

Cross Tick

↓

Current Active Liquidity

4
```

Question:

> **How did the Active Liquidity suddenly change from 14 to 4?**

Did the pool go through every LP again?

Did it recalculate all liquidity positions from scratch?

**No.**

Instead,

it performs one very small update.

This lesson explains exactly how that happens.

# Why Is This Even A Problem?

Let's imagine there are only three LPs.

```text
Alice

$4 ---------------- $7

Liquidity = 10
```

```text
Bob

$7 ---------------- $10

Liquidity = 5
```

```text
Charlie

$7 ---------------- $15

Liquidity = 8
```

Suppose the current price is

```text
$5
```

Only Alice is active.

So,

```text
Current Active Liquidity

=

10
```

Now imagine the price increases until

```text
$8
```

Alice is no longer active.

Bob and Charlie become active.

Now,

```text
Current Active Liquidity

=

5 + 8

=

13
```

Easy.

But now imagine something much bigger.

Instead of

3 LPs...

Imagine

```text
1,000,000 Liquidity Providers.
```

Every time the price moves,

should Uniswap check

```text
Alice?

Bob?

Charlie?

David?

...

1,000,000 LPs...
```

every single swap?

❌ Absolutely not.

That would be extremely slow and expensive.

So Uniswap asks a much smarter question.

Instead of asking

> **Who is active now?**

it asks

> **What changed?**

That single idea leads us to `liquidityNet`.

# What Is `liquidityNet`?

The lesson immediately defines it.

Notice the wording carefully.

It does **NOT** say:

> "`liquidityNet` is the current Active Liquidity."

Instead, it says

> **When the current price crosses an initialized tick, we either add or subtract some amount from the current Active Liquidity.**

That amount is called

```text
ΔL
```

or

```text
liquidityNet
```

Think of it like this.

```text
Current Active Liquidity

↓

Cross Tick

↓

Apply The Stored Change

↓

New Active Liquidity
```

That stored change is

```text
liquidityNet
```

It is **not**

the Active Liquidity itself.

It is simply

> **the amount by which the Active Liquidity should change.**

It simply tells the pool

That stored value

is called

```text
liquidityNet
```

# Why Store **+L** And **-L**?

> **📖 Note**
>
> In this section, we're using the simplest possible example—a **single Liquidity Provider (LP)**—to build an intuition for why `tickLower` stores `+L` and `tickUpper` stores `-L`.
>
> The previous note, **`Ticks, Tick Boundaries & liquidityNet.md`**, covers **all major scenarios**, including:
>
> - Single LP
> - Multiple LPs
> - Overlapping LPs
> - Price moving Left (←)
> - Price moving Right (→)
> - Entering a liquidity range
> - Leaving a liquidity range
> - How Active Liquidity changes in each case
>
> Here, we're intentionally focusing on the simplest case so that the intuition behind `+L` and `-L` becomes crystal clear before moving on to more complex scenarios.

This is one of the biggest questions people have when first learning Uniswap V3.

The lesson tells us:

At

```text
tickLower
```

store

```text
+L
```

At

```text
tickUpper
```

store

```text
-L
```

Most people immediately ask:

> **Why?**

Don't memorize it.

Let's derive it together.

---

## Imagine One LP

Suppose Alice provides liquidity between

```text
$4 --------------------------- $7

Liquidity = 10
```

This means Alice's liquidity only exists between

```text
$4 → $7
```

Outside this range,

her liquidity does not exist.

---

## Question 1

When should Alice become active?

Answer:

When the current price **enters** her range.

Where does the price enter her range?

```text
$4
```

So,

when the price crosses

```text
tickLower ($4)
```

the pool should increase the current Active Liquidity.

Therefore,

the pool stores

```text
+10
```

at

```text
tickLower
```

because liquidity is being **added**.

---

## Question 2

When should Alice stop being active?

Answer:

When the current price **leaves** her range.

Where does that happen?

```text
$7
```

So,

when the price crosses

```text
tickUpper ($7)
```

the pool should decrease the current Active Liquidity.

Therefore,

the pool stores

```text
-10
```

at

```text
tickUpper
```

because liquidity is being **removed**.

Suddenly,

the signs make perfect sense.

You are no longer memorizing

```text
+L

-L
```

Instead,

you're thinking

```text
Entering a range

↓

Add liquidity

↓

+L
```

```text
Leaving a range

↓

Remove liquidity

↓

-L
```

# Direction

Now comes one of the cleverest parts of the design.

The price can move in two directions.

It can move

```text
← Left
```

or

```text
Right →
```

Instead of creating different logic for each situation,

Uniswap simply stores the direction as a number.

If the price increases,

```text
Direction = +1
```

If the price decreases,

```text
Direction = -1
```

That's it.

Now the same formula can work for both directions.

No special cases.

No complicated logic.

---

## 👶 Child Analogy — Walking Through A Door

Imagine a room with a single door.

You can either

walk **into** the room

or

walk **out of** the room.

The same door is used.

The only thing that changes is

your direction.

Exactly the same happens in Uniswap.

The tick stays the same.

Only the direction changes.

```text
Price moves Right

↓

Direction = +1
```

```text
Price moves Left

↓

Direction = -1
```

Later,

the pool combines this direction with the stored

```text
liquidityNet
```

to determine whether the current Active Liquidity should increase or decrease.

## 🧠 Before We Continue...

At this point, you should already understand the following:

- Only **initialized ticks** store `liquidityNet`.
- `tickLower` stores **+L**.

# The Liquidity Update Formula

The lesson gives us the following formula.

```text
Next Liquidity

=

Current Liquidity

+

(Direction × liquidityNet)
```

At first glance,

this formula can look confusing.

Don't memorize it.

Let's understand **why it works.**

---

# Example 1 — Price Moves Left (←)

Suppose Alice has provided liquidity between

```text
$4 ------------------------ $7

Liquidity = 10
```

The current Active Liquidity is

```text
10
```

Now imagine a swap happens.

The price starts moving **left**.

Eventually,

the price crosses

```text
tickLower ($4)
```

Remember,

the lower tick stores

```text
+10
```

because Alice's liquidity **begins** there.

The price moved left,

so the direction is

```text
Direction = -1
```

Now apply the formula.

```text
Next Liquidity

=

Current Liquidity

+

(Direction × liquidityNet)

=

10

+

(-1 × 10)

=

0
```

The result is

```text
0
```

Exactly what we expected.

---

# Why Did It Become Zero?

Think about Alice's liquidity.

It only exists between

```text
$4 ------------------------ $7
```

Now imagine the price moves below

```text
$4
```

```text
Current Price

↓

$3.9
```

Alice's range has ended.

The price is no longer inside her liquidity range.

Therefore,

Alice should no longer contribute any liquidity.

So,

Active Liquidity becomes

```text
0
```

This matches the formula perfectly.

---

# 👶 Child Analogy — Leaving A Playground

Imagine a playground.

Only children **inside the playground** count.

Alice is inside.

```text
Children Inside = 1
```

Now Alice walks outside the gate.

Should we still count her?

❌ No.

Now

```text
Children Inside = 0
```

Nothing magical happened.

She simply left the area that we're counting.

Liquidity behaves exactly the same way.

Once the price leaves Alice's range,

her liquidity is no longer counted.

Exactly what we expected.

Alice's liquidity is no longer active because the price has moved beyond her range.


Alice walks out through the exit.

Now,

```text

```


# The Beautiful Pattern

Notice something elegant.

The lower boundary stores

```text
+L
```

The upper boundary stores

```text
-L
```

The direction tells the pool

whether that stored value should increase or decrease the current Active Liquidity.

Because of this,

the **same formula**

```text
Next Liquidity

=

Current Liquidity

+

(Direction × liquidityNet)
```

works for **every situation**.

Whether the price moves

- Left (←)
- Right (→)

Whether there is

- One LP
- Two LPs
- Hundreds of LPs
- Thousands of overlapping LPs

the pool always performs the same calculation.

No special cases.

No giant `if/else` statements.

Just one elegant formula.

---

# 🎯 The Big Idea

Don't think of `liquidityNet` as

> **"Alice's liquidity."**

Instead,

think of it as

> **"The amount by which the current Active Liquidity should change whenever this initialized tick is crossed."**

That small change,

combined with the direction of price movement,

is enough for the pool to instantly know the next Active Liquidity.

This is why Uniswap V3 never needs to recalculate every Liquidity Provider during every swap.
