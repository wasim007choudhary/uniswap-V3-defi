# Deriving the Equation for the Curve of Real Reserves

# Part 1

This lesson is one of the most important mathematical lessons in Uniswap V3.

At first glance, the equations look intimidating.

However, the actual objective of this lesson is surprisingly simple.

It answers one question:

> **How do we move from the Virtual Constant Product Curve to the Real Reserve Curve?**

Do **not** try to memorize the final equation.

Instead, understand **why** every step exists.

---

# Why Do We Need Another Equation?

Until now, we've been using the familiar Constant Product Equation:

```text
X × Y = L²
```

Question:

**What does this equation actually represent?**

Does it represent the actual amount of Token X and Token Y inside our Position?

**No.**

It represents the **Virtual Reserve Curve.**

---

# Didn't We Already Derive X and Y?

Yes.

Previously we derived:

```text
X = L / √P
```

and

```text
Y = L√P
```

These equations describe points on the **Virtual Constant Product Curve.**

---

# Why Isn't The Virtual Curve Enough?

Remember how concentrated liquidity works.

Unlike Uniswap V2,

our liquidity does **not** exist across every possible price.

Instead,

our Position only exists between:

```text
Pa

↓

Pb
```

Outside this range,

our liquidity is inactive.

Therefore,

the infinite Constant Product Curve cannot completely describe our Position.

We need another curve that represents **only the liquidity inside our chosen range.**

That curve is called the:

```text
Real Reserve Curve
```

# Additional Discussion — What Is the Main Purpose of the Real Reserve Curve Equation?

After deriving the Real Reserve Curve equation, an important question came up:

> **What is the main purpose of this equation?**

The equation is:

```text
(Xr + L/√Pb)(Yr + L√Pa) = L²
```

The short answer is:

> **Its primary purpose is to describe and represent the Real Reserve Curve.**

---

# Starting Point — The Virtual Curve

We begin with the familiar constant product equation:

```text
X × Y = L²
```

This equation describes the **Virtual Curve**.

One important thing to remember is that this virtual curve assumes liquidity exists everywhere.

In other words, it extends from:

```text
Price = 0

↓

∞
```

There is no beginning or end.

Liquidity is assumed to exist at every possible price.

---

# The Problem

Uniswap V3 does **not** work this way.

Liquidity Providers choose a specific price range.

For example:

```text
Pa ------------------------- Pb
```

Liquidity only exists inside this range.

Outside this range, the position becomes inactive.

Therefore,

the Virtual Curve no longer correctly represents what actually exists inside the pool.

We need another curve.

---

# The Solution — Translate the Curve

Instead of changing the shape of the constant product curve,

Uniswap V3 simply **moves** it.

Specifically,

the Virtual Curve is translated:

- Left by:

```text
L / √Pb
```

- Down by:

```text
L√Pa
```

After applying these exact translations,

the curve becomes the **Real Reserve Curve**.

This translated curve is described by:

```text
(Xr + L/√Pb)(Yr + L√Pa) = L²
```

---

# Why Exactly These Translation Amounts?

During the derivation, we found:

```text
Xv = L / √Pb
```

and

```text
Yv = L√Pa
```

These are **not arbitrary numbers.**

They are the exact horizontal and vertical distances required to move the Virtual Curve so that it perfectly aligns with the Real Reserve Curve.

Not more.

Not less.

Exactly these amounts.

---

# Numerical Example — Translating the Virtual Curve into the Real Reserve Curve

To better understand why the translation amounts are:

```text
L / √Pb
```

and

```text
L√Pa
```

let's work through a complete numerical example.

---

## Step 1 — Start with the Virtual Curve

Suppose:

```text
L = 10
```

Since:

```text
X × Y = L²
```

we get:

```text
X × Y = 10²

=

100
```

So our Virtual Curve is:

```text
X × Y = 100
```

---

## Step 2 — Define the Price Range

Suppose the liquidity Position is active between:

```text
Pa = 1

Pb = 4
```

Taking square roots:

```text
√Pa = 1
```

```text
√Pb = 2
```

---

## Step 3 — Calculate the Translation Amounts

### Horizontal Translation

We previously derived:

```text
Xv = L / √Pb
```

Substitute the values:

```text
10 / 2

=

5
```

Therefore,

```text
Xv = 5
```

---

### Vertical Translation

We also derived:

```text
Yv = L√Pa
```

Substitute:

```text
10 × 1

=

10
```

Therefore,

```text
Yv = 10
```

---

## Step 4 — Build the Real Reserve Curve

Substitute these values into:

```text
(Xr + Xv)(Yr + Yv) = L²
```

Result:

```text
(Xr + 5)(Yr + 10) = 100
```

This is now the equation describing the **Real Reserve Curve** for this liquidity Position.

# What Is This Equation Actually Used For?

The immediate purpose of the equation is to mathematically describe the **Real Reserve Curve**.

However,

it is also the mathematical foundation for many later equations in Uniswap V3.

it becomes possible to derive:

- Amount of Token X inside a price range.
- Amount of Token Y inside a price range.
- Generalized token amount equations.
- Swap mathematics.
- Liquidity mathematics.
- Reserve calculations as the price changes.

In other words,

this equation acts as the bridge between the geometric idea of concentrated liquidity and the practical formulas used throughout the rest of Uniswap V3.
